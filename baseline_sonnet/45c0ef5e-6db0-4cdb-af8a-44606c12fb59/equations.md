# Abstract to equations

Status: **partial**

Model: `claude-sonnet-5-5`

Mathematics has not been independently verified.

Implement a universal one- and two-qubit gate set on two exchange-coupled electron spins with constant non-diagonal exchange and small g-factor difference, using magnetic-field amplitude control (a few Gauss) and optimal control to find the evolution.

Scope: Smallest useful construction: a standard two-spin Heisenberg-exchange Hamiltonian with a Zeeman term, a rotating-frame resonant drive on one spin, and a generic gate-fidelity cost for optimal control. The actual control fields, the non-diagonal exchange form, the suppression scheme via repeated single-spin rotations, the optimal-control algorithm and the numerical results are outside this scope.

## Symbols

- $J$: exchange coupling constant; domain: real, J>0 assumed
- $g_1,g_2$: electron g-factors with small difference; domain: positive reals
- $\mu_B$: Bohr magneton; domain: positive constant
- $B(t)$: magnetic field amplitude along z, controlled over a few Gauss; domain: real-valued function of time
- $\sigma^{(k)}_{\alpha}$: Pauli operator on spin k; domain: alpha in {x,y,z}, k in {1,2}
- $U_{target}$: target one- or two-qubit gate; domain: 4x4 unitary
- $T$: total gate duration; domain: positive real
- $F$: gate fidelity used as optimal-control objective; domain: [0,1]

## eq_1: Two-spin Hamiltonian

$$
H(t)=\frac{\mu_B B(t)}{2}\left(g_1\sigma^{(1)}_z+g_2\sigma^{(2)}_z\right)+\frac{J}{4}\,\vec\sigma^{(1)}\cdot\vec\sigma^{(2)}
$$

Generic Heisenberg exchange between two electron spins with a Zeeman term in which the g-factors differ slightly, giving different resonance frequencies.

Provenance: `assumption`. The abstract mentions constant exchange and a g-factor difference but gives no explicit Hamiltonian. The isotropic form is a placeholder and may differ from the paper's non-diagonal form.

Equation assumptions:

- Isotropic exchange
- hbar=1
- Static field plus control amplitude in B(t)

## eq_2: Selective resonance condition

$$
\omega_k=g_k\mu_B B_0/\hbar,\quad \Delta\omega=(g_1-g_2)\mu_B B_0/\hbar
$$

Each spin has its own Larmor frequency, so a drive tuned to omega_k addresses spin k. The small difference Delta omega enables addressing.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard Zeeman resonance; consistent with the abstract's claim that a g-factor difference allows addressing qubits.

Equation assumptions:

- Static field B_0
- Weak exchange relative to Zeeman splitting is not guaranteed

## eq_3: Time evolution

$$
U(T)=\mathcal{T}\exp\left(-i\int_0^T H(t)\,dt\right)
$$

Time-ordered propagator generated by the controlled Hamiltonian.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard quantum dynamics for the evolution that the optimal control determines.

Equation assumptions:

- hbar=1

## eq_4: Gate fidelity objective

$$
F=\frac{1}{16}\left|\mathrm{Tr}\left(U_{target}^{\dagger}U(T)\right)\right|^{2}
$$

Phase-insensitive gate fidelity to be maximized over the control B(t).

Provenance: `assumption`. The abstract says optimal control theory is used to find the evolution but does not state the cost functional. This is a common choice.

Equation assumptions:

- Global phase ignored
- Computational subspace is the full 4-dimensional space

## Registers

- q1: 1 qubits; spin of electron 1 as qubit 1
- q2: 1 qubits; spin of electron 2 as qubit 2

## Classical postprocessing

Classical optimization loop over the control amplitudes (algorithm unspecified in the abstract).

## Algorithm steps

1. Define the two-spin Hamiltonian with constant exchange and a field B(t) acting on both spins with different g-factors. (eq_1, eq_2)
2. Parametrize B(t) as piecewise constant with amplitude variations of a few Gauss, bringing spin 1 and then spin 2 into resonance. (eq_2)
3. Propagate the system under the control field to obtain U(T). (eq_3)
4. Optimize the control parameters to maximize fidelity with the target one- or two-qubit gate. (eq_4)
