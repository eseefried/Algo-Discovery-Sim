# Abstract to equations

Status: **partial**

Model: `claude-fable-5-1`

Mathematics has not been independently verified.

Continuous-time quantum walk (CTQW) spatial search for a single marked site on a two-dimensional honeycomb (graphene) lattice, exploiting the Dirac point (E=0) of the tight-binding spectrum, with the marked site identified by a modified (non-standard) local perturbation of the walk Hamiltonian rather than only an on-site potential.

Scope: One smallest useful construction: nearest-neighbour tight-binding CTQW on an n x n periodic honeycomb lattice with N = 2 n^2 sites, n = 3 (N = 18 sites, so that Dirac-point momenta are allowed), a single marked site w, a bond-plus-on-site marking perturbation with free strengths, an initial state built from the E = 0 (Dirac-point) Bloch eigenstates, time evolution, and computational-basis measurement to estimate the success probability at w as a function of time. Outside scope: the paper's specific catalogue of 'various ways of marking a site', the paper's asymptotic scaling claims (search time / success probability versus N), the comparison showing failure of standard marking on square lattices, and the amplitude-transfer (communication) protocol between two sites.

## Symbols

- $n$: Linear size of the periodic honeycomb lattice in unit cells; n must be a multiple of 3 for the Dirac points to be allowed momenta; domain: positive integer, 3 | n; instance n = 3
- $N$: Total number of lattice sites, N = 2 n^2; domain: positive integer; instance N = 18
- $(m_1, m_2, \sigma)$: Site label: unit-cell coordinates m_1, m_2 and sublattice index sigma (A = 0, B = 1); domain: m_1, m_2 \in \mathbb{Z}_n, \sigma \in \{0,1\}
- $\gamma$: Nearest-neighbour hopping strength of the unperturbed walk Hamiltonian; domain: \gamma > 0 (set \gamma = 1 in the instance)
- $H_0$: Unperturbed tight-binding (adjacency-type) CTQW Hamiltonian on the honeycomb lattice; domain: Hermitian N x N matrix
- $w$: Marked (target) site; domain: w \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}; instance w = index of (0,0,A) = 0
- $\mathcal{N}(w)$: Set of the three nearest neighbours of w; domain: subset of sites, |\mathcal{N}(w)| = 3
- $\epsilon, \mu$: Strengths of the bond modification and on-site potential forming the marking perturbation; domain: real parameters to be scanned
- $H_w$: Marked (search) Hamiltonian; domain: Hermitian N x N matrix
- $\theta_1, \theta_2$: Bloch phases along the two lattice directions, \theta_i = 2\pi q_i / n; domain: q_i \in \mathbb{Z}_n
- $t$: Evolution time; domain: t \ge 0
- $P_w(t)$: Probability of finding the walker at the marked site at time t; domain: [0,1]
- $\mathrm{idx}$: Integer encoding of a site into the computational basis of the site register; domain: \mathrm{idx}(m_1,m_2,\sigma) = 2(n m_1 + m_2) + \sigma \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}

## eq_1: Unperturbed graphene CTQW Hamiltonian

$$
H_0 = -\gamma \sum_{m_1, m_2 \in \mathbb{Z}_n} \sum_{\delta \in \{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}} \left( |m_1, m_2, A\rangle\langle m_1 - \delta_1, m_2 - \delta_2, B| + \mathrm{h.c.} \right)
$$

Nearest-neighbour hopping on the periodic honeycomb lattice: site (m_1,m_2,A) couples to B sites in cells (m_1,m_2), (m_1-1,m_2), (m_1,m_2-1), indices taken mod n. This is the walk generator (Laplacian and adjacency generators coincide up to a constant on this 3-regular graph).

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. A CTQW on a graphene lattice is generated by the nearest-neighbour tight-binding/adjacency Hamiltonian of the honeycomb graph; the abstract names the walk type and the lattice but not the explicit matrix, so this is an inference.

Abstract evidence: continuous-time quantum walk search is possible in two-dimensions by changing the search topology to a graphene lattice

Equation assumptions:

- Periodic boundary conditions (torus) assumed
- Uniform hopping gamma and zero on-site energy assumed

## eq_2: Spectrum and Dirac point of H_0

$$
E_{\pm}(\theta_1,\theta_2) = \pm \gamma \left| 1 + e^{-i\theta_1} + e^{-i\theta_2} \right|, \quad \theta_i = \frac{2\pi q_i}{n}, \qquad E_{\pm} = 0 \iff (\theta_1,\theta_2) \in \left\{ \left(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}, -\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\right), \left(-\tfrac{2\pi}{3}, \tfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) \right\}
$$

Two-band Bloch spectrum of the honeycomb tight-binding model; the two bands touch at E = 0 at the two inequivalent Dirac points K, K', which are allowed momenta exactly when 3 divides n. At these momenta the sublattice-pure Bloch states |K,A>, |K,B>, |K',A>, |K',B> are exact zero-energy eigenstates of H_0.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard tight-binding result for graphene; the abstract states that the Dirac point is utilised but gives no formula.

Equation assumptions:

- Periodic boundary conditions
- n divisible by 3

## eq_3: Marked (search) Hamiltonian with modified local marking

$$
H_w = H_0 + \epsilon \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(w)} \left( |w\rangle\langle j| + |j\rangle\langle w| \right) + \mu\, |w\rangle\langle w|
$$

The marked site is distinguished by changing the three bonds attached to it (hopping -gamma becomes -gamma + epsilon) and optionally adding an on-site potential mu. The standard CTQW marking corresponds to epsilon = 0, mu != 0; the abstract states that a change to this standard marking is required and that several variants work.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. The abstract asserts that a non-standard local marking is used and that several variants succeed; a local perturbation acting on the marked site and its bonds is the natural general form. The specific (epsilon, mu) values and the exact family of perturbations used in the paper are not given in the abstract and are treated as a parameter scan.

Abstract evidence: This is made possible by making a change to standard methods of marking a particular site in the lattice. Various ways of marking a site are shown to result in successful search protocols.

Equation assumptions:

- Perturbation is supported on w and its three bonds only
- Specific values of epsilon and mu are not established by the abstract; the instance scans epsilon in (0, gamma] with mu in {0, -gamma}

## eq_4: Initial state at the Dirac point

$$
|\psi(0)\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2}{N}} \sum_{m_1, m_2 \in \mathbb{Z}_n} \cos\!\left( \frac{2\pi (m_1 - m_2)}{3} \right) \left( |m_1, m_2, A\rangle + |m_1, m_2, B\rangle \right), \qquad H_0 |\psi(0)\rangle = 0
$$

A real, normalised, equal-weight superposition of the four zero-energy Dirac-point Bloch states (K and K', both sublattices). It lies in the E = 0 eigenspace of H_0, so the search dynamics are driven by the coupling of the perturbation to the Dirac-point states rather than to the band bottom (which the uniform superposition would occupy).

Provenance: `assumption`. The abstract says the Dirac point is utilised but does not specify the starting state; this is one concrete choice of a delocalised zero-energy state. Normalisation: (2/N) * 2 * sum_cells cos^2 = (2/N) * 2 * (n^2/2) = 1 for 3 | n.

Equation assumptions:

- The paper's actual starting state may differ (e.g. a single Dirac Bloch state or a differently weighted combination)

## eq_5: Time evolution and success probability

$$
|\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-i H_w t} |\psi(0)\rangle, \qquad P_w(t) = \left| \langle w | \psi(t) \rangle \right|^2, \qquad t_{\mathrm{opt}} = \arg\max_{t \in [0, T]} P_w(t)
$$

Continuous-time evolution under the marked Hamiltonian and the probability of locating the walker at the marked site; the search time is the first (or global, within a finite window T) maximum of P_w(t). Probability on the neighbours N(w) may also be monitored since a bond perturbation can localise amplitude on w and its neighbours jointly.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Definition of CTQW search success probability; no closed form for t_opt or max P_w is claimed here since the abstract gives no scaling result.

Equation assumptions:

- Finite time window T chosen numerically, e.g. T = 10 sqrt(N)/gamma for the instance

## eq_6: Site-to-qubit encoding for the instance

$$
\mathrm{idx}(m_1, m_2, \sigma) = 2(n m_1 + m_2) + \sigma, \qquad |m_1, m_2, \sigma\rangle \mapsto |\mathrm{idx}\rangle_{\lceil \log_2 N \rceil}, \qquad \langle k | H_w | l \rangle = 0 \ \text{for } k \ge N \text{ or } l \ge N
$$

Binary encoding of the N = 18 sites into a 5-qubit register (little-endian integer), with the 14 unused basis states decoupled (zero rows/columns of H_w).

Provenance: `assumption`. Needed so that the equations map unambiguously onto a circuit; the paper describes a physical walk, not a qubit encoding.

Equation assumptions:

- Binary (not one-hot) encoding chosen for minimal qubit count

## Registers

- site: ceil(log2 N) = 5 qubits for N = 18 qubits; Holds the walker position |idx(m_1,m_2,sigma)> in binary; amplitudes on basis states 18..31 are identically zero

## Initial state

|psi(0)> as in eq_4: the normalised real Dirac-point superposition sqrt(2/N) sum_{m1,m2} cos(2 pi (m1 - m2)/3) (|m1,m2,A> + |m1,m2,B>), prepared on the site register via a generic 18-amplitude state preparation (amplitudes take values in {+sqrt(2/N), -sqrt(2/N)/2}).

## Measurement

Computational-basis measurement of the 5-qubit site register; the empirical frequency of outcome idx(w) = 0 estimates P_w(t). Optionally also record frequencies of the three neighbour indices.

## Classical postprocessing

For each perturbation setting (epsilon, mu), locate the time t_opt maximising the estimated P_w(t) over the scanned grid and report (t_opt, P_w(t_opt)); compare against the unperturbed dynamics (epsilon = mu = 0), for which P_w(t) stays at O(1/N), and against the standard on-site-only marking (epsilon = 0) to check that bond modification is what enables localisation. No asymptotic scaling in N is claimed from the single instance.

## Algorithm steps

1. Fix the instance: n = 3, N = 18, gamma = 1, periodic honeycomb adjacency per eq_1, marked site w = idx(0,0,A) = 0 with neighbours N(w) = {idx(0,0,B), idx(2,0,B), idx(0,2,B)}; build H_w for chosen (epsilon, mu) per eq_3 and embed it in the 5-qubit space per eq_6. (eq_1, eq_3, eq_6)
2. Prepare the Dirac-point initial state |psi(0)> (eq_4) on the site register; verify classically that H_0 |psi(0)> = 0 using eq_2. (eq_2, eq_4)
3. Apply U(t) = exp(-i H_w t) for a grid of times t in [0, T] (exact unitary synthesis or a Trotter product of the bond terms; each bond term is a 2-level X-type coupling between two computational basis states). (eq_5)
4. Measure the site register in the computational basis and estimate P_w(t) (and optionally the total probability on N(w)); repeat the time scan for each (epsilon, mu) in the chosen parameter set and record t_opt and max P_w. (eq_5, eq_6)
