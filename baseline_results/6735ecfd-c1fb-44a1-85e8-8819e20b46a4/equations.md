# Abstract to equations

Status: **partial**

Model: `claude-fable-5-1`

Mathematics has not been independently verified.

Approximate the single-qubit Markovian channel T_t = e^{tL} generated by a Lindblad generator L, to trace-norm accuracy epsilon, using a circuit of single-qubit and CNOT gates acting on the system qubit plus one ancilla qubit, via a decomposition of L into simple pieces whose channels are implemented individually and recombined by a product (Suzuki-type) formula.

Scope: One smallest useful construction: a two-term splitting L = L_1 + L_2 of a single-qubit Lindbladian (coherent X-rotation term plus amplitude damping), a second-order symmetric recombination formula with n steps, and a one-ancilla Stinespring dilation of the amplitude-damping step. Tiny instance: omega = 1, gamma = 0.5, t = 1, n = 2, input state rho_0 = |+><+|. Outside scope: the paper's general method for approximating an arbitrary single-qubit channel with one ancilla (the specific 'recently developed methods' are not reproduced), the general proof of the O((||L||t)^{3/2}/epsilon^{1/2}) gate count, choice of decomposition for arbitrary generators, and gate-level Solovay-Kitaev compilation of the dilation unitaries.

## Symbols

- $\rho$: density operator of the system qubit; domain: positive semidefinite 2x2 complex matrices with unit trace
- $\mathcal{L}$: Lindblad (GKSL) generator of the semigroup; domain: linear superoperator on 2x2 complex matrices
- $T_t$: Markovian channel at time t, T_t = e^{t\mathcal{L}}; domain: completely positive trace-preserving map on 2x2 matrices, t \ge 0
- $\|\mathcal{L}\|_{(1\to 1)}$: induced trace-norm (Schatten-1 to Schatten-1) norm of the generator; domain: non-negative real
- $\epsilon$: target accuracy in induced trace norm; domain: (0,1)
- $n$: number of recombination (Trotter) steps; domain: positive integer
- $\tau$: time step, \tau = t/n; domain: positive real
- $H$: system Hamiltonian; instance H = (\omega/2) X; domain: 2x2 Hermitian matrix
- $\gamma$: amplitude-damping rate; domain: non-negative real
- $\sigma_-$: lowering operator |0\rangle\langle 1|; domain: 2x2 complex matrix
- $p$: damping probability of one dissipative step, p = 1-e^{-\gamma\tau}; domain: [0,1]
- $K_i$: Kraus operators of one dissipative step; domain: 2x2 complex matrices, i \in \{0,1\}
- $U_{\mathrm{AD}}$: two-qubit dilation unitary implementing the amplitude-damping step; domain: 4x4 unitary on ancilla \otimes system

## eq_1: Lindblad generator and semigroup

$$
T_t = e^{t\mathcal{L}},\qquad \mathcal{L}(\rho) = -i[H,\rho] + \sum_k \left( L_k \rho L_k^\dagger - \tfrac{1}{2}\{L_k^\dagger L_k, \rho\} \right)
$$

The channel to be simulated is the exponential of a generator of GKSL form; the semigroup property T_{s+t}=T_sT_t holds exactly.

Provenance: `abstract_explicit`. The abstract states T_t = e^{tL} for a semigroup of channels; the GKSL form of L is the standard characterization of a generator of a quantum dynamical semigroup (background, not stated in the abstract).

Abstract evidence: approximates the channel $T_{t}=e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ up to chosen accuracy $\epsilon$

Equation assumptions:

- Generator is time-independent (Markovian semigroup).

## eq_2: Induced trace norm of the generator

$$
\|\mathcal{L}\|_{(1\to 1)} = \sup_{X \neq 0} \frac{\|\mathcal{L}(X)\|_1}{\|X\|_1}
$$

Norm appearing in the gate-count bound; distance between channels is measured in the same induced norm.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard definition of the (1->1) induced superoperator norm matching the subscript used in the abstract's complexity statement.

## eq_3: Decomposition of the generator (instance)

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_1 + \mathcal{L}_2,\quad \mathcal{L}_1(\rho) = -i\left[\tfrac{\omega}{2}X, \rho\right],\quad \mathcal{L}_2(\rho) = \gamma\left(\sigma_-\rho\sigma_+ - \tfrac{1}{2}\{\sigma_+\sigma_-,\rho\}\right)
$$

Split into a coherent piece and a single dissipative piece, each of whose exponentials is an exactly implementable channel; the two pieces do not commute for omega, gamma > 0.

Provenance: `assumption`. The abstract says a 'decomposition and recombination technique' is used but does not specify the decomposition; this two-term amplitude-damping-plus-X-rotation instance is a chosen minimal non-commuting example.

Equation assumptions:

- Instance parameters omega = 1, gamma = 0.5.
- Arbitrary generators would require the paper's general decomposition, not reconstructed here.

## eq_4: Second-order recombination formula and error

$$
S_2(\tau) = e^{\tau\mathcal{L}_1/2}\, e^{\tau\mathcal{L}_2}\, e^{\tau\mathcal{L}_1/2},\qquad \left\| T_t - S_2(t/n)^n \right\|_{(1\to 1)} \le C\,\frac{(\|\mathcal{L}\|_{(1\to 1)}\, t)^3}{n^2} \le \epsilon \;\Rightarrow\; n = \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{(\|\mathcal{L}\|_{(1\to 1)} t)^{3/2}}{\epsilon^{1/2}}\right)
$$

Symmetric Suzuki-type product formula for completely positive semigroups; the inequality is an approximation bound, not an equality. C is a constant depending on the number of terms in the decomposition.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. Inferred: the stated scaling (||L||t)^{3/2}/epsilon^{1/2} is exactly what a second-order symmetric product formula with O(1) gates per step gives (first order would give (||L||t)^2/epsilon), together with the abstract's 'decomposition and recombination technique ... inspired by developments in Hamiltonian simulation'.

Abstract evidence: requires only $\mathcal{O}\big((||\mathcal{L}||_{(1\rightarrow 1)}t)^{3/2}/\epsilon^{1/2}\big)$ single qubit and CNOT gates

Equation assumptions:

- Each factor e^{tau L_j} is implemented exactly (or to error negligible compared with epsilon/n) with O(1) gates.
- The constant C is not given by the abstract.

## eq_5: Amplitude-damping step and its Kraus form

$$
e^{\tau\mathcal{L}_2}(\rho) = K_0\rho K_0^\dagger + K_1\rho K_1^\dagger,\quad K_0 = |0\rangle\langle 0| + \sqrt{1-p}\,|1\rangle\langle 1|,\quad K_1 = \sqrt{p}\,|0\rangle\langle 1|,\quad p = 1 - e^{-\gamma\tau}
$$

Exact closed form of the dissipative step for the chosen instance; two Kraus operators, so a single ancilla qubit suffices for its dilation.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard solution of the amplitude-damping Lindbladian; exact equality.

Equation assumptions:

- Applies only to the instance generator eq_3.

## eq_6: One-ancilla Stinespring dilation

$$
e^{\tau\mathcal{L}_2}(\rho) = \mathrm{Tr}_A\!\left[ U_{\mathrm{AD}} \left(|0\rangle\langle 0|_A \otimes \rho\right) U_{\mathrm{AD}}^\dagger \right],\qquad U_{\mathrm{AD}} = \mathrm{CNOT}_{A\to S}\cdot \mathrm{C}_S\!\left[R_y(\theta)\right]_A,\quad \theta = 2\arcsin\sqrt{p}
$$

Circuit realisation: a system-controlled R_y(theta) on the ancilla followed by a CNOT from ancilla to system, with the ancilla prepared in |0>; the ancilla is traced out (or measured and discarded). Register order: ancilla A, system S, with (<i|_A ⊗ I) U (|0>_A ⊗ I) = K_i.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. The abstract states one ancilla suffices; for the instance channel (two Kraus operators) the standard amplitude-damping dilation circuit with one ancilla is exact. The paper's general one-ancilla method for arbitrary single-qubit channels is not reconstructed.

Abstract evidence: the algorithm requires only a single ancilla qubit, the minimal possible dilation for a non-unitary single-qubit quantum channel

Equation assumptions:

- Ancilla is reset to |0> before each dissipative step (or a fresh ancilla is used); the abstract does not specify reuse.
- Controlled-R_y decomposes into O(1) single-qubit and CNOT gates.

## Registers

- S: 1 qubits; system qubit carrying rho, the state whose Markovian evolution is simulated
- A: 1 qubits; single ancilla qubit, prepared in |0> and traced out after each dissipative step

## Initial state

|0\rangle_A \otimes \rho_0 with \rho_0 = |+\rangle\langle +|_S (instance choice); the ancilla is in |0> at the start of every dissipative step.

## Measurement

For verification only: perform single-qubit state tomography on S (measure X, Y, Z expectation values over repeated runs) to estimate the output state S_2(t/n)^n(rho_0). The ancilla may be measured in the computational basis and the outcome discarded.

## Classical postprocessing

Compare the tomographically reconstructed output with the exact e^{tL}(rho_0) computed classically for the 2x2 instance, and check the trace distance is at most epsilon (plus statistical error). Compute n from the bound in eq_4.

## Algorithm steps

1. Fix t, epsilon; choose n from the second-order bound (instance: n = 2) and set tau = t/n. (eq_2, eq_4)
2. Repeat n times: apply the half coherent step e^{(tau/2) L_1} as the unitary R_x(omega tau/2) on S. (eq_3, eq_4)
3. Within each step: prepare/reset A to |0>, apply U_AD (system-controlled R_y(theta) on A, then CNOT A->S) with p = 1 - e^{-gamma tau}, then discard/trace out A. (eq_5, eq_6)
4. Within each step: apply the second half coherent step R_x(omega tau/2) on S, completing S_2(tau). (eq_3, eq_4)

## Assumptions

- No experiment_scope supplied; the instance (X-rotation plus amplitude damping, omega=1, gamma=0.5, t=1, n=2, rho_0=|+><+|) is a chosen minimal construction, not from the abstract.
- The recombination formula is taken to be second-order symmetric Suzuki splitting, inferred from the stated complexity scaling.
- The single ancilla is reused by resetting to |0> between dissipative steps.
- Each exponentiated piece is implemented exactly; the abstract's general approximation of arbitrary single-qubit channels (which may involve convex combinations of extreme channels and Solovay-Kitaev compilation) is replaced here by an exactly dilatable two-Kraus channel.
