# Abstract to equations

Status: **partial**

Model: `claude-fable-5-1`

Mathematics has not been independently verified.

Decide whether two given connected (n+2)-regular cospectral graphs, G_4(n;n+2) and G_5(n;n+2) (and the related T_4, T_5), are non-isomorphic by (i) comparing their adjacency operators rewritten in the antisymmetric (fermionic) multiparticle basis of a continuous-time quantum walk and (ii) comparing ground-state entanglement entropies of harmonic-oscillator networks defined on the graphs.

Scope: Scoped to ONE construction: a two-fermion (antisymmetric two-particle) continuous-time quantum walk generated by the graph adjacency matrix, applied to a pair of N-vertex k-regular cospectral graphs with adjacency matrices A and A', with the smallest parameter n=1 (k=3) as the nominal tiny instance. The explicit vertex sets/edge sets of G_4(n;n+2), G_5(n;n+2), T_4, T_5 are not given in the abstract and are left as missing inputs. The harmonic-oscillator entanglement-entropy test is given only as a standard Gaussian-state background formula with an assumed coupling matrix; its specific bipartition ('two special parts') and the analytic closed-form entropies of the paper remain outside scope. Walks with more than two particles, the T_4/T_5 constructions, and the paper's analytic proofs are outside scope.

## Symbols

- $G,\;G'$: The two graphs of a cospectral pair (e.g. G_4(n;n+2) and G_5(n;n+2)); domain: simple connected k-regular graphs on N vertices, k=n+2
- $N$: Number of vertices of each graph; domain: positive integer (unknown function of n for G_4, G_5)
- $A,\;A'$: Adjacency matrices of G and G' (vertices labelled 0..N-1); domain: symmetric 0/1 matrices in \{0,1\}^{N\times N}, zero diagonal, row sums k
- $P$: Permutation matrix implementing a vertex relabeling; domain: N\times N permutation matrices
- $\lambda_i$: Eigenvalues of A (single-particle walk energies); domain: real, i=0..N-1
- $|ij\rangle_-$: Antisymmetric (two-fermion) basis state with particles on vertices i<j; domain: orthonormal basis of the \binom{N}{2}-dimensional antisymmetric subspace of \mathbb{C}^N\otimes\mathbb{C}^N
- $A^{(2)}$: Adjacency (walk) operator rewritten in the antisymmetric fermionic basis; domain: real symmetric \binom{N}{2}\times\binom{N}{2} matrix
- $t$: Walk evolution time; domain: real, t\ge 0
- $K$: Potential (coupling) matrix of the harmonic-oscillator network; domain: real symmetric positive-definite N\times N matrix
- $S_{\mathcal A}$: Von Neumann entanglement entropy of the oscillator subset \mathcal A in the network ground state; domain: real, S_{\mathcal A}\ge 0
- $\nu_m$: Symplectic eigenvalues of the reduced covariance matrix of subset \mathcal A; domain: real, \nu_m\ge 1/2

## eq_1: Graph isomorphism as vertex relabeling

$$
G \cong G' \iff \exists P \in S_N:\; A' = P A P^{T}
$$

Two graphs are isomorphic iff their adjacency matrices are related by simultaneous row/column permutation.

Provenance: `abstract_explicit`. Direct matrix statement of the relabeling definition given in the abstract.

Abstract evidence: Two graph are isomorphic when they are related to each other by a relabeling of the graph vertices.

Equation assumptions:

- Vertices labelled 0..N-1 so that A is an N x N matrix.

## eq_2: Cospectrality of the non-isomorphic pair

$$
\det(\lambda I - A) = \det(\lambda I - A') \;\forall \lambda \quad\text{while}\quad \nexists P:\; A' = P A P^{T}
$$

The pair has identical adjacency spectra (so single-particle quantum walks have identical energy spectra) yet is non-isomorphic.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. 'Cospectral' is standardly taken with respect to the adjacency matrix; the abstract asserts both cospectrality and non-isomorphism.

Abstract evidence: These two graphs are a pair of non-isomorphic connected cospectral regular graphs for any positive integer $n$.

Equation assumptions:

- Cospectrality refers to the adjacency spectrum (not Laplacian), consistent with the adjacency-based walk used in the paper.

## eq_3: Adjacency in the antisymmetric two-fermion basis

$$
|ij\rangle_- = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(|i\rangle|j\rangle - |j\rangle|i\rangle\big),\; i<j;\qquad A^{(2)}_{(ij),(kl)} = {}_-\langle ij|\,(A\otimes I + I\otimes A)\,|kl\rangle_- = \delta_{jl}A_{ik} + \delta_{ik}A_{jl} - \delta_{jk}A_{il} - \delta_{il}A_{jk}
$$

Matrix elements of the two-particle hopping operator restricted to the antisymmetric (fermionic) subspace; this is the smallest multiparticle version of 'rewriting the adjacency matrix in the antisymmetric fermionic basis'.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. The abstract names the antisymmetric fermionic basis; the explicit two-particle, non-interacting form is the minimal standard realisation. The paper may use more particles or additional terms.

Abstract evidence: we rewrite the adjacency matrices of graphs in the antisymmetric fermionic basis

Equation assumptions:

- Exactly two particles (smallest nontrivial multiparticle case).
- Non-interacting particles: the two-particle generator is A⊗I + I⊗A with no interaction term.

## eq_4: Two-fermion continuous-time walk and its spectrum

$$
U^{(2)}(t) = e^{-i t A^{(2)}},\qquad p_t(kl\,|\,ij) = \big|{}_-\langle kl|U^{(2)}(t)|ij\rangle_-\big|^2,\qquad \mathrm{spec}\big(A^{(2)}\big) = \{\lambda_a+\lambda_b : a<b\}
$$

Walk unitary generated by the antisymmetric-basis adjacency and the resulting pair-occupation distribution. For non-interacting fermions the two-particle spectrum is determined by the single-particle spectrum, so cospectral pairs (eq_2) yield identical two-particle spectra; any distinguishing power must come from comparing the matrices/dynamics beyond the spectrum, as the paper's criterion (not stated in the abstract) presumably does.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard continuous-time quantum walk definition and the standard spectral decomposition of a non-interacting two-particle Hamiltonian on the antisymmetric subspace.

Equation assumptions:

- Hamiltonian is the adjacency matrix (not the Laplacian) with unit hopping and \hbar=1.
- Non-interacting two-fermion model as in eq_3.

## eq_5: Harmonic-oscillator network ground state (assumed coupling)

$$
H_{\mathrm{osc}} = \tfrac12\sum_{i} p_i^2 + \tfrac12\, x^{T} K x,\quad K = \omega_0^2 I + L,\; L = kI - A;\qquad \Sigma_x = \tfrac12 K^{-1/2},\; \Sigma_p = \tfrac12 K^{1/2}
$$

Nodes as identical unit-mass quantum harmonic oscillators coupled along edges; the ground state is Gaussian with position/momentum covariance matrices fixed by K.

Provenance: `assumption`. The abstract states nodes are identical harmonic oscillators but does not give the coupling matrix; the Laplacian coupling with an on-site frequency \omega_0>0 (to make K positive definite) is a common choice and is proposed here as an explicit assumption.

Equation assumptions:

- Unit masses, \hbar=1.
- Coupling matrix K = \omega_0^2 I + L with \omega_0 > 0; the paper's actual K (e.g. adjacency-based or different normalisation) is unknown.

## eq_6: Entanglement entropy of a node subset from symplectic eigenvalues

$$
\nu_m = \sqrt{\mathrm{eig}_m\big((\Sigma_x)_{\mathcal A}(\Sigma_p)_{\mathcal A}\big)},\qquad S_{\mathcal A} = \sum_{m=1}^{|\mathcal A|}\Big[(\nu_m+\tfrac12)\ln(\nu_m+\tfrac12) - (\nu_m-\tfrac12)\ln(\nu_m-\tfrac12)\Big]
$$

Von Neumann entropy of the reduced Gaussian state of oscillator subset \mathcal A (one 'part' of the network); the GI test compares S_{\mathcal A}(G) with S_{\mathcal A'}(G') for corresponding parts.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard formula for the entropy of a Gaussian state in terms of symplectic eigenvalues of the reduced covariance matrix; the abstract says entropy between two parts of the network is computed but does not specify the parts or the closed form.

Equation assumptions:

- Pure Gaussian ground state so that S of one part equals the entanglement entropy of the bipartition.
- Choice of subset \mathcal A ('two special parts') unspecified by the abstract.

## Registers

- v1: \lceil \log_2 N \rceil qubits; Vertex position of fermion 1 (integer i encoded in binary, qubit 0 = least significant bit)
- v2: \lceil \log_2 N \rceil qubits; Vertex position of fermion 2 (integer j encoded in binary, qubit 0 = least significant bit)

## Initial state

|\psi_0\rangle = |i_0 j_0\rangle_- = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|i_0\rangle_{v1}|j_0\rangle_{v2} - |j_0\rangle_{v1}|i_0\rangle_{v2}) for a chosen adjacent or fixed vertex pair i_0<j_0 (same labels used for both graphs); the choice of (i_0,j_0) is an assumption.

## Measurement

Computational-basis measurement of registers v1 and v2 after U^{(2)}(t), giving vertex pairs (k,l); outcomes with k=l have zero probability for an ideal antisymmetric state. Repeat to estimate p_t(kl|i_0 j_0) for each graph.

## Classical postprocessing

Compare the estimated distributions p_t(·|i_0 j_0) for G and G' (e.g. total-variation distance over all choices of initial pair and times used); report 'non-isomorphic' if they differ beyond statistical error for some (i_0,j_0,t) under a fixed labelling convention, noting that the abstract's actual comparison criterion ('adjacency matrices ... are different') is not specified. Separately, compute S_{\mathcal A} from eq_5–eq_6 for each graph and compare.

## Algorithm steps

1. Classically build A and A' for the pair (G,G') and form the antisymmetric-basis operators A^{(2)} and A'^{(2)} (eq_3); verify cospectrality of A and A' (eq_2) so that single-particle spectra cannot distinguish them. (eq_2, eq_3)
2. Prepare the antisymmetric two-particle initial state |i_0 j_0\rangle_- on registers v1,v2. (eq_3)
3. For each graph, apply the two-fermion walk unitary U^{(2)}(t) = exp(-i t A^{(2)}) (Hamiltonian simulation of A⊗I + I⊗A, which preserves the antisymmetric subspace) for one or more times t. (eq_4)
4. Measure v1,v2 in the computational basis to estimate the pair-occupation distribution p_t(kl|i_0 j_0) for each graph; compare the two distributions (and/or the matrices A^{(2)}, A'^{(2)} directly) as the GI witness. (eq_4)
5. (Classical, outside the quantum circuit) Build the oscillator coupling K for each graph, compute reduced covariance matrices of the chosen subset \mathcal A, and compare entanglement entropies S_{\mathcal A}(G) vs S_{\mathcal A'}(G'). (eq_5, eq_6)

## Assumptions

- No experiment_scope was supplied; the smallest construction chosen is a two-fermion non-interacting continuous-time walk generated by the adjacency matrix, with nominal tiny instance n=1 (3-regular G_4(1;3), G_5(1;3)).
- Two particles only; the paper's 'multiparticle' walk may use more particles.
- Hamiltonian = adjacency matrix with unit hopping, \hbar=1.
- Oscillator coupling matrix taken as K = \omega_0^2 I + L with \omega_0>0 (eq_5); the paper's actual oscillator Hamiltonian is unknown.
- Initial vertex pair (i_0,j_0) and the entropy bipartition \mathcal A are free choices not fixed by the abstract.
- Caveat: for non-interacting fermions, spec(A^{(2)}) is fixed by spec(A), so cospectral graphs give isospectral two-fermion walks; distinguishing them requires comparing the full operators/dynamics, not spectra.
