# Abstract to equations

Status: **partial**

Model: `claude-fable-5-1`

Mathematics has not been independently verified.

Estimate the Trotter step size (and hence number of Trotter steps) required so that a Trotter-Suzuki approximation of the time-evolution operator of a second-quantized electronic-structure Hamiltonian yields a ground-state energy accurate to a target precision, comparing the actual ground-state energy error against the standard operator-norm commutator bound that the abstract reports can be loose by many orders of magnitude.

Scope: Scoped construction (chosen as an assumption since no experiment_scope was given): a single first-order Trotter step U_T(\Delta t) for a tiny molecular Hamiltonian (H2 in a minimal STO-3G basis, N=4 spin orbitals, Jordan-Wigner encoding on 4 qubits), together with the effective Hamiltonian H_eff(\Delta t) it implements, the resulting ground-state energy error \Delta E(\Delta t), and the comparison of \Delta E with the operator-norm bound. The optional embedding of U_T in phase estimation is described but not required. Outside scope: the paper's numerical survey over many molecules, the claimed 16-orders-of-magnitude looseness result, the chemistry-based heuristics (maximum nuclear charge, orbital filling fraction) for predicting cost, the specific classical pre-processing strategies to reduce step size or estimate step count, and the improved (asymptotically superior) state-preparation methods; none of these are mathematically specified in the abstract and are not reconstructed here.

## Symbols

- $N$: number of spin orbitals (= number of system qubits under Jordan-Wigner); domain: positive integer; N=4 in the scoped instance
- $a_p^\dagger, a_p$: fermionic creation/annihilation operators for spin orbital p; domain: operators on the fermionic Fock space, p \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}
- $h_{pq}, h_{pqrs}$: one- and two-electron molecular integrals in the chosen orbital basis (Hartree); domain: real numbers (real orbitals assumed)
- $H$: second-quantized electronic-structure Hamiltonian; domain: Hermitian operator on (\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes N}
- $H_j$: j-th term in a decomposition H=\sum_{j=1}^{L} H_j into terms each of which is efficiently exponentiable (one Pauli string or one fermionic term after Jordan-Wigner); domain: Hermitian operators, j=1,\dots,L
- $L$: number of Hamiltonian terms in the decomposition; domain: positive integer
- $\Delta t$: Trotter time step; domain: positive real (atomic units of time)
- $U_T(\Delta t)$: first-order Trotter approximation of e^{-iH\Delta t}; domain: unitary on (\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes N}
- $H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta t)$: effective Hamiltonian exactly generated by the Trotter unitary, U_T(\Delta t)=e^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}\Delta t}; domain: Hermitian operator
- $E_0, E_0^{T}(\Delta t)$: exact ground-state energy of H and ground-state energy of H_eff (the energy phase estimation of U_T would return); domain: real numbers (Hartree)
- $\Delta E(\Delta t)$: ground-state energy error induced by Trotterization; domain: non-negative real
- $\epsilon$: target energy accuracy (chemical accuracy); domain: positive real; assumed \epsilon = 1.6\times10^{-3} Hartree
- $Z_{\max}, \nu$: maximum nuclear charge in the molecule and filling fraction (number of electrons divided by N), the chemical descriptors the abstract argues control cost; domain: Z_{\max} positive integer; \nu \in [0,1]

## eq_1: Second-quantized electronic-structure Hamiltonian

$$
H=\sum_{p,q=0}^{N-1} h_{pq}\,a_p^\dagger a_q+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p,q,r,s=0}^{N-1} h_{pqrs}\,a_p^\dagger a_q^\dagger a_r a_s=\sum_{j=1}^{L}H_j
$$

Standard molecular Hamiltonian in a basis of N spin orbitals, written as a sum of L terms; after a Jordan-Wigner transformation (a_p \mapsto \tfrac12(X_p+iY_p)\prod_{k<p}Z_k, qubit k encodes occupation of spin orbital k, qubit 0 least significant) each H_j is a sum of commuting Pauli strings.

Provenance: `standard_background`. The abstract refers to simulation of quantum chemistry and scaling with the number of spin orbitals; this is the textbook Hamiltonian such simulations Trotterize. The integrals h_{pq},h_{pqrs} are not given in the abstract.

Equation assumptions:

- Born-Oppenheimer, non-relativistic electronic Hamiltonian with real orbitals
- Jordan-Wigner encoding with occupation of spin orbital k stored on qubit k

## eq_2: First-order Trotter step and operator-norm error bound

$$
U_T(\Delta t)=\prod_{j=1}^{L}e^{-iH_j\Delta t},\qquad \left\|e^{-iH\Delta t}-U_T(\Delta t)\right\|\le\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}\sum_{j<k}\left\|[H_j,H_k]\right\|
$$

Lie-Trotter product formula (terms applied in the fixed order j=1,\dots,L) and the standard spectral-norm bound on the per-step error operator; this is the type of bound the abstract says prior work used.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Recalled textbook Trotter error bound; the abstract states prior work bounded Trotter errors in terms of the norm of the error operator, which is what this inequality formalizes, but the abstract does not write the formula itself.

Equation assumptions:

- First-order (Lie-Trotter) formula; higher-order Suzuki formulas are out of scope
- Spectral (operator) norm

## eq_3: Effective Hamiltonian generated by the Trotter step

$$
U_T(\Delta t)=e^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta t)\Delta t},\qquad H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta t)=H-\frac{i\Delta t}{2}\sum_{j<k}[H_j,H_k]+O(\Delta t^{2})
$$

Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion: the Trotter unitary is exactly the evolution under a Hermitian effective Hamiltonian whose leading correction is first order in \Delta t. For the tiny instance H_eff can be obtained exactly as (i/\Delta t)\log U_T on the principal branch.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard BCH identity; it is the natural tool for turning Trotter error into a ground-state energy error, which the abstract treats as the relevant figure of merit.

Equation assumptions:

- \Delta t small enough that the principal matrix logarithm of U_T is well defined (\|H\|\Delta t<\pi)

## eq_4: Ground-state energy error due to Trotterization

$$
E_0^{T}(\Delta t)=\min\operatorname{spec}H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta t)=-\frac{1}{\Delta t}\arg\lambda_0\big(U_T(\Delta t)\big),\qquad \Delta E(\Delta t)=\left|E_0^{T}(\Delta t)-E_0\right|
$$

The energy that phase estimation on U_T would return is the ground eigenvalue of H_eff (eigenphase of U_T divided by \Delta t); the Trotter-induced ground-state error is its deviation from the exact ground energy E_0 of H. This is an exact equality for the eigenvalue, not a bound.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. The abstract uses 'ground state error' as the quantity of interest for small systems; the only Trotter-induced ground-state energy error is the shift of the ground eigenvalue of the effective Hamiltonian, so this definition is inferred as the quantity being studied.

Abstract evidence: numerical results for small systems fail to reveal any clear correlation between ground state error and number of spin orbitals

Equation assumptions:

- \lambda_0 denotes the eigenvalue of U_T adiabatically connected to the ground state of H (no branch crossing for the chosen \Delta t)
- Energy error (not state-fidelity error) is the figure of merit

## eq_5: Rigorous bound on the energy error and its looseness ratio

$$
\Delta E(\Delta t)\le\left\|H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta t)-H\right\|\le\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{j<k}\left\|[H_j,H_k]\right\|+O(\Delta t^{2})=:B(\Delta t),\qquad R(\Delta t)=\frac{B(\Delta t)}{\Delta E(\Delta t)}\ge1
$$

Weyl's eigenvalue perturbation inequality gives the first bound; the second is the commutator-norm bound. The ratio R quantifies how loose the norm-based bound is relative to the actual ground-state error; the abstract claims R can reach ~10^{16} for some molecules, a result not reproducible from the abstract alone.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. The abstract compares norm-based error bounds to actual errors and reports their looseness; this equation formalizes that comparison using standard perturbation inequalities. The numerical value 10^{16} is the abstract's claim, not derived here.

Abstract evidence: we find that these error bounds can be loose by up to sixteen orders of magnitude for some molecules

Equation assumptions:

- Comparison at fixed \Delta t and for the leading-order commutator bound; the paper may use a differently normalized bound

## eq_6: Required Trotter step size and step count for target accuracy (scoped instance)

$$
\Delta t^{*}=\max\{\Delta t>0:\ \Delta E(\Delta t)\le\epsilon\},\qquad n_{\mathrm{steps}}=\left\lceil\frac{t}{\Delta t^{*}}\right\rceil,\qquad \epsilon=1.6\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{Ha}
$$

Definition of the empirically required step size from the actual energy error (rather than from the bound B), and the resulting number of Trotter steps for total evolution time t (e.g. the time needed by phase estimation to resolve \epsilon, t\sim\pi/\epsilon). The ceiling makes this an integer, not an exact ratio.

Provenance: `assumption`. The abstract motivates estimating the necessary number of steps using classical processing; this is a concrete, minimal operationalization (direct numerical evaluation of \Delta E on a tiny instance), not the paper's specific strategy, which is not described in the abstract.

Equation assumptions:

- Chemical accuracy \epsilon = 1.6\times10^{-3} Hartree as target
- Monotone growth of \Delta E in \Delta t over the searched range
- Tiny instance: H2/STO-3G at bond length 0.7414 \AA, N=4, 2 electrons (\nu=1/2)

## Registers

- sys: N = 4 qubits; System register encoding spin-orbital occupations under Jordan-Wigner (qubit k = occupation of spin orbital k, qubit 0 least significant); U_T(\Delta t) acts here
- anc: m (optional; m \approx \lceil\log_2(\pi/(\epsilon\Delta t))\rceil+2 if phase estimation is performed) qubits; Optional phase-estimation register storing the eigenphase of U_T; not needed for the classical evaluation of \Delta E on the tiny instance

## Initial state

System register in a Hartree-Fock reference determinant with 2 occupied spin orbitals, |\psi_{HF}\rangle = a_1^\dagger a_0^\dagger|0\rangle \mapsto |0011\rangle (qubits 3..0), assumed to have non-negligible overlap with the ground state; ancilla register (if used) in |0\rangle^{\otimes m}.

## Measurement

Not required for the scoped classical evaluation. If the optional phase-estimation embedding is used: measure the m-qubit ancilla register in the computational basis after controlled-U_T^{2^k} applications and an inverse QFT, giving an m-bit estimate \phi of the eigenphase, with E_0^T \approx 2\pi\phi/\Delta t modulo 2\pi/\Delta t (branch chosen using a classical energy estimate).

## Classical postprocessing

Compute \Delta E(\Delta t)=|E_0^T(\Delta t)-E_0| and the bound B(\Delta t)=(\Delta t/2)\sum_{j<k}\|[H_j,H_k]\|; report R=B/\Delta E; locate \Delta t^* by bisection on \Delta E(\Delta t)\le\epsilon and output n_{steps}=\lceil t/\Delta t^*\rceil (integer ceiling, not an exact ratio). All quantities for the N=4 instance are computable by exact classical linear algebra.

## Algorithm steps

1. Classically compute the molecular integrals h_{pq}, h_{pqrs} for the tiny instance, build H as a sum of L Jordan-Wigner Pauli-string terms H_j in a fixed order, and compute the exact ground energy E_0 (exact diagonalization is trivial for N=4). (eq_1)
2. For a chosen \Delta t, construct the first-order Trotter step U_T(\Delta t) as the ordered product of exponentials e^{-iH_j\Delta t} (each a Pauli-string rotation circuit) and, in classical post-analysis, the commutator-norm bound B(\Delta t). (eq_2, eq_5)
3. Obtain the effective Hamiltonian H_eff(\Delta t) (exactly via the matrix logarithm of U_T, or to leading order via BCH) and its ground eigenvalue E_0^T(\Delta t); equivalently, on hardware, run phase estimation of U_T on |\psi_{HF}\rangle and read the eigenphase. (eq_3, eq_4)
4. Compute the actual ground-state Trotter error \Delta E(\Delta t) and the looseness ratio R(\Delta t)=B/\Delta E; scan \Delta t to find the largest step \Delta t^* satisfying \Delta E\le\epsilon and the implied step count n_steps. (eq_4, eq_5, eq_6)
