# Abstract to equations

Status: **partial**

Model: `claude-fable-5-1`

Mathematics has not been independently verified.

Continuous-time quantum walk (CTQW) spatial search on a graph, analysed by reducing the dynamics to a small invariant (Krylov) subspace generated by the Lanczos algorithm from the marked vertex; the abstract claims this yields new optimal-search instances including complete graphs with broken links and complete bipartite graphs (star graph).

Scope: ONE smallest useful construction chosen (no experiment_scope supplied): CTQW spatial search on the star graph K_{1,N-1} with the marked vertex being a leaf, reduced via Lanczos from |w> to a 3-dimensional invariant subspace, with a tiny explicit instance N=4 (center vertex 0, leaves 1,2,3, marked vertex w=1) encoded on 2 qubits. Outside scope: complete graphs with broken links, general complete bipartite graphs K_{m,n}, quantum transport efficiency calculations, link-removal efficiency improvements, and the XY spin-network single-qubit transfer fidelity bound. The paper's specific choice of Hamiltonian (adjacency vs Laplacian), optimal hopping rate gamma and search time are not stated in the abstract and are treated as assumptions/missing.

## Symbols

- $G=(V,E)$: Undirected graph on which the walk takes place; here the star graph K_{1,N-1}; domain: finite simple graph, |V|=N
- $N$: Number of vertices (Hilbert space dimension of the walker); domain: positive integer; instance N=4
- $A$: Adjacency matrix of G, A_{uv}=1 iff (u,v) in E; domain: N x N symmetric 0/1 matrix
- $\gamma$: Hopping rate of the CTQW; domain: positive real
- $|w\rangle$: Marked (target) vertex state; domain: computational basis state of the vertex register; instance w=1
- $|c\rangle$: Center vertex state of the star graph; domain: computational basis state; instance c=0
- $|r\rangle$: Normalised uniform superposition over unmarked leaves; domain: unit vector in C^N
- $|s\rangle$: Uniform superposition over all vertices (initial state); domain: unit vector in C^N
- $H$: Search Hamiltonian; domain: N x N Hermitian matrix
- $H_{\mathrm{red}}$: Search Hamiltonian restricted to the Lanczos invariant subspace; domain: 3 x 3 real symmetric matrix
- $\alpha_k,\beta_k$: Lanczos diagonal and off-diagonal coefficients; domain: real numbers
- $t$: Evolution time; domain: non-negative real
- $p(t)$: Success probability of measuring the marked vertex at time t; domain: [0,1]

## eq_1: CTQW spatial search Hamiltonian

$$
H = -\gamma A - |w\rangle\langle w|
$$

Standard Childs–Goldstone continuous-time spatial search Hamiltonian with adjacency-matrix walk term and marked-vertex oracle term.

Provenance: `standard_background`. The abstract names the spatial quantum search algorithm but gives no formula; this is the standard CTQW search Hamiltonian. Whether the paper uses the adjacency matrix or the Laplacian is not stated.

Equation assumptions:

- Adjacency matrix (not Laplacian) is used for the walk term
- Single marked vertex

## eq_2: Initial uniform state

$$
|s\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{v\in V}|v\rangle
$$

Walker starts in the equal superposition over all N vertices.

Provenance: `standard_background`. Standard initial state of CTQW spatial search.

## eq_3: Lanczos recursion generating the invariant subspace

$$
|v_0\rangle=|w\rangle,\quad \beta_{k+1}|v_{k+1}\rangle = H|v_k\rangle - \alpha_k|v_k\rangle - \beta_k|v_{k-1}\rangle,\quad \alpha_k=\langle v_k|H|v_k\rangle,\ \beta_k=\langle v_{k-1}|H|v_k\rangle,\quad \beta_{m}=0 \Rightarrow \mathcal{K}=\mathrm{span}\{|v_0\rangle,\dots,|v_{m-1}\rangle\},\ H\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
$$

Three-term Lanczos recursion started from the marked vertex; termination (vanishing beta) yields an H-invariant Krylov subspace in which H is tridiagonal.

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. The abstract states the invariant subspace is computed with the Lanczos algorithm; the recursion itself is the standard Lanczos iteration. Starting vector |w> is an assumption (the exact Hamiltonian/starting vector used in the paper is not given).

Abstract evidence: we use invariant subspace methods, that can be computed systematically using Lanczos algorithm, to obtain the reduced set of states that encompass the dynamics of the problem at hand without the specific knowledge of underlying symmetries

Equation assumptions:

- Lanczos is seeded with |w> and applied to the search Hamiltonian H (or equivalently to A, since |w><w| preserves the Krylov space of A seeded at |w>)
- Exact arithmetic (no loss of orthogonality)

## eq_4: Reduced basis for the star graph with a marked leaf

$$
|w\rangle,\quad |c\rangle,\quad |r\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-2}}\sum_{v\in V\setminus\{c,w\}}|v\rangle;\qquad |s\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(|w\rangle+|c\rangle+\sqrt{N-2}\,|r\rangle\right)
$$

Lanczos from |w> on the star graph terminates after three vectors; the initial state |s> lies in this subspace, so the full dynamics is confined to it.

Provenance: `assumption`. Proposed concrete realisation of the reduction for the star graph (abstract: 'complete bipartite graphs, in particular, the star graph'); follows from A|w>=|c> and A|c>=|w>+sqrt(N-2)|r>, but the paper's specific reduced basis is not quoted in the abstract.

Equation assumptions:

- Marked vertex is a leaf, not the center
- N >= 3 so that |r> is well defined

## eq_5: Reduced search Hamiltonian in basis (|w>,|c>,|r>)

$$
H_{\mathrm{red}} = -\gamma\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&\sqrt{N-2}\\0&\sqrt{N-2}&0\end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

Exact (not approximate) 3x3 restriction of H to the invariant subspace of eq_4; dynamics of |s> under H equals dynamics of its 3-component coordinate vector under H_red.

Provenance: `assumption`. Direct computation from eq_1 and eq_4 for the star graph under the stated assumptions; not a relation quoted from the abstract.

Equation assumptions:

- eq_1 (adjacency form) and eq_4 hold

## eq_6: Success probability and optimality criterion

$$
p(t)=\left|\langle w|e^{-iHt}|s\rangle\right|^2 = \left|\langle w|e^{-iH_{\mathrm{red}}t}|s\rangle\right|^2,\qquad \text{optimal search:}\ \exists\,\gamma,\ t^*=O(\sqrt{N})\ \text{with}\ p(t^*)=\Theta(1)
$$

Probability of finding the marked vertex after time t; search is called optimal if a constant success probability is reached in time scaling as sqrt(N).

Provenance: `abstract_inferred`. The abstract claims optimality of spatial search on the star graph; 'optimal' is interpreted in the standard sense of Grover-like O(sqrt N) time with Theta(1) success probability. The specific values of gamma and t* for the star graph are not given in the abstract.

Abstract evidence: new instances of graphs where the spatial quantum search algorithm is optimal: complete graphs with broken links and complete bipartite graphs, in particular, the star graph

Equation assumptions:

- Standard definition of optimal spatial search

## Registers

- vertex: n = log2(N) qubits; 2 qubits for the N=4 instance qubits; Encodes the walker position; vertex index v in {0,...,N-1} stored in binary (big-endian: qubit 0 is the most significant bit). Vertex 0 is the star center, vertices 1..N-1 are leaves, marked vertex w=1.

## Initial state

|s> = H^{\otimes 2}|00> = (1/2)(|0>+|1>+|2>+|3>) on the 2-qubit vertex register (eq_2), equal to (1/2)(|w>+|c>+sqrt(2)|r>) in the reduced basis for N=4.

## Measurement

Computational-basis measurement of all vertex-register qubits; outcome bit string decoded as integer v; success iff v = w (=1, i.e. bit string 01 for N=4).

## Classical postprocessing

Estimate p(t*) as the fraction of shots yielding v=w; compare to the exact value obtained from exp(-i H_red t*) (eq_6) to validate the reduced-subspace prediction.

## Algorithm steps

1. Classical preprocessing: build A for the star graph K_{1,N-1}, form H (eq_1), run Lanczos from |w> (eq_3) to obtain the 3-dimensional invariant basis (eq_4) and H_red (eq_5); choose gamma and evolution time t* by maximising p(t) from eq_6 using the 3x3 matrix (values not supplied by the abstract). (eq_1, eq_3, eq_4, eq_5, eq_6)
2. Prepare the uniform superposition over vertices with Hadamards on every qubit of the vertex register. (eq_2)
3. Apply the unitary U(t*) = exp(-i H t*) on the vertex register (for N=4 this is a 4x4 unitary that can be compiled exactly; for larger N a Trotter approximation of exp(i gamma A t) and exp(i |w><w| t) would introduce approximation error). (eq_1, eq_6)
4. Measure the vertex register in the computational basis and record whether the outcome equals w. (eq_6)

## Assumptions

- No experiment_scope was supplied; the star graph with a marked leaf and N=4 was chosen as the single smallest useful instance
- Search Hamiltonian uses the adjacency matrix with a projector oracle (eq_1); the paper may instead use the graph Laplacian
- Marked vertex is a leaf; marking the center gives a different (2-dimensional) reduction
- gamma and t* are to be determined numerically from H_red; no paper values are available from the abstract
- Exact unitary synthesis of exp(-iHt) for the 4x4 instance; Trotterisation is not analysed
