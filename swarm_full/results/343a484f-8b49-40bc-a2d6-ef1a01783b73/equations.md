Simulate an arbitrary Markovian (semigroup) qubit channel T_t = e^{tL} generated by L, approximating it to accuracy epsilon with single-qubit and CNOT gates and a single ancilla qubit, using a decomposition and recombination technique built on methods for approximating arbitrary single-qubit channels.

Scope: Abstract-level reconstruction only: the problem statement, gate-complexity scaling, and dilation claim. The explicit decomposition, recombination, and channel-approximation circuits are not given in the abstract and are not reconstructed.

## Symbols

- $T_t$: single-qubit quantum channel at time t in the semigroup; domain: CPTP maps on a qubit, t>=0
- $\mathcal{L}$: generator of the Markovian semigroup; domain: linear maps on 2x2 matrices (Lindbladian)
- $\|\mathcal{L}\|_{(1\to 1)}$: induced 1-to-1 norm of the generator; domain: nonnegative real
- $\epsilon$: target approximation accuracy; domain: (0,1)
- $t$: evolution time; domain: nonnegative real

## eq_1: Semigroup channel

$$
T_t = e^{t\mathcal{L}}
$$

The channel to be approximated is the exponential of the generator.

Provenance: `abstract_explicit`. Stated directly in the abstract.

Abstract evidence: approximates the channel $T_{t}=e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ up to chosen accuracy $\epsilon$

## eq_2: Gate complexity

$$
N_{\rm gates}=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{(\|\mathcal{L}\|_{(1\to1)}t)^{3/2}}{\epsilon^{1/2}}\right)
$$

Number of single-qubit and CNOT gates required.

Provenance: `abstract_explicit`. Stated directly in the abstract.

Abstract evidence: $\mathcal{O}\big((||\mathcal{L}||_{(1\rightarrow 1)}t)^{3/2}/\epsilon^{1/2}\big)$ single qubit and CNOT gates

## Registers

- system: 1 qubits; qubit on which the channel T_t acts
- ancilla: 1 qubits; single dilation qubit for the non-unitary channel, reset or discarded after each segment

## Initial state

System qubit in an arbitrary input state rho; ancilla in a fixed reference state (assumed |0>).

## Measurement

The abstract specifies no measurement. Assumed: the ancilla is traced out (discarded or reset) and the system qubit is read out in a chosen basis (e.g. Pauli X, Y, Z) over repeated runs, or full state tomography is performed, to compare the output with T_t(rho).

## Classical postprocessing

Assumed: estimate expectation values of Pauli observables from the measurement counts and reconstruct the output Bloch vector. Compare it with the exact e^{tL}(rho), to within accuracy epsilon, as a check. No postprocessing is given in the abstract.

## Algorithm steps

1. Decompose the evolution time into segments (e.g. T_t = (T_{t/n})^n) and decompose each short-time channel into simpler pieces, in the spirit of Hamiltonian-simulation decomposition; the exact decomposition is not given in the abstract. (eq_1)
2. Approximate each piece as an arbitrary single-qubit channel using the single-ancilla dilation, implemented with single-qubit gates and CNOTs. (eq_2)
3. Recombine the approximated pieces sequentially, reusing the single ancilla, to approximate T_t to accuracy epsilon with the stated gate count. (eq_1, eq_2)
